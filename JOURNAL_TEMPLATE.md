# JOURNAL.md — Decision Journal

Copy this file into your repo as `JOURNAL.md` and fill it in **as you work**, not at the end. Short honest notes beat polished essays. Bullet points are fine. We read this before we read your code.

One rule: **be honest.** "AI wrote most of this and I fixed two bugs" is a fine answer if it's true. We check the journal against the code and against your interview answers, and a made-up journal shows fast.

## 1. Prioritization

- What did you decide to build, in what order, and why?
- What did you deliberately cut or skip? Why those?

## 2. Key decisions

For each decision that shaped the project (aim for 3–6), one short block:

- **Decision:** what you chose (e.g. how the widget embeds, what happens to rejected testimonials, DB choice).
- **Options:** what else you considered.
- **Why:** why you picked this one.

Include the calls you made where the brief was silent.

## 3. Working with AI agents

- **Tools and models used:** which agent/editor (Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, Cline, aider, …), which models, and for what kind of work.
- **How you split the work:** what tasks you gave the agent, what you kept for yourself, and why.
- **Your agent setup:** if you wrote instruction/rules files, skills, or commands (they must be committed — see the brief), say briefly what each is for and what problem it solved. If you used none, say so here.
- **Your 3–5 most important prompts:** paste them and say why each worked — or didn't.
- **At least one time AI was wrong:** what it produced, how you noticed, what you did. If AI was never wrong in 6+ hours of use, say so — and expect us to ask about it.
- **Something you rejected:** AI output you threw away or heavily rewrote, and why.

## 4. Verification

- How did you convince yourself the code actually works? Be specific: what did you run, click, test, or inspect?
- What do you know is still broken or fragile?

## 5. If I had 5 more hours

- What would you do next, in order?
